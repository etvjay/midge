# Private voting: how to close the five shortfalls

*2026-11-07. Ten deep-research runs (Parallel `pro` and Tavily `pro`, two per shortfall). The sources are cited by those tools; I read their summaries and recommendation sections, not every cited paper. Where a line is my own reasoning rather than the research, it says so.*

| Shortfall | What the research says | What Midge does about it | Cost and status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Ballots not tied to an account** (GAP-023) | Sign the *ciphertext plus its election context*; encryption alone does not authenticate a sender. Verify that the signing key belongs to the account, not just that the signature is valid (NEAR accounts hold many removable keys; NEP-413 specifies signed messages). Anonymous credentials (Semaphore, BBS+) or blind signatures hide who voted but need a trusted issuer or a ZK setup. | Each ballot is signed by the member's NEAR key over (ciphertext, syndicate, epoch, round nonce). The room checks on chain that the key is on that account, and refuses anything else. | Small. Build first. Also closes the auction's bid-authentication gap. Hides the choice, not who voted. |
| **2. One person, one vote** | A nullifier gives one use per *credential*; it cannot show two credentials are two people. Identity must be secured at enrolment. For a known committee, a reviewed roster plus secret ballots is the proportionate design. For DAOs add Passport/BrightID/Proof of Humanity/World ID at enrolment. Quadratic voting makes Sybils pay, so it needs stronger identity. | The roll is fixed in the release policy when the room is hatched, one account per member. Midge claims "one account, one vote, one deposit", never "one person". Personhood checks, if wanted, happen before enrolment, outside Midge. | No code. State the limit. |
| **3. Nobody can verify the count** | Remote attestation proves the code, not that the tally is right. Strongest practical pattern: publish an append-only transcript; bind the election id and transcript digest into the attestation report data (TDX gives 64 bytes); verify measurements against a pinned image; redundant independent tallying; MACI-style ZK tally proofs are the gold standard but heavy. TEEs are not standalone proofs of a correct tally. | (a) Publish the list of ballot hashes before tallying, and give every voter an inclusion receipt. (b) The room signs the result over the transcript digest. (c) Two independent rooms (the Phala TDX room and a NEAR AI model-sealed room) tally and **both** must sign; the contract checks both. | Medium. (c) needs a contract change (two enclave signatures). Honest result: a single-room tally is attestation-trusted; two rooms need two failures to lie. |
| **4. Coercion and vote-buying** | Encryption gives neither receipt-freeness nor coercion resistance. MACI's key change and re-voting make a shown ballot unreliable evidence of the final vote; it assumes an honest coordinator. Threshold encryption does not stop a voter keeping their opening witness. Hardware can also help the *buyer* (an attested vote-selling enclave). Revoting fails if the coercer controls the voter continuously. | Allow re-voting: the latest signed ballot per account replaces earlier ones, and the window stays open until close. Reduces, does not remove, vote-buying. For committee votes this is a lesser threat; say so. | Small. Partial by design. |
| **5. Leaks (turnout, unanimity)** | A unanimous result defeats unlinkability even when everything is encrypted. Dummy ballots and fixed anonymity sets help only in larger groups. For tiny boards, cryptography alone cannot hide unanimity; use winner-only publication, combined reporting, delayed reveal, or accept it. | Everyone on the roll must deposit and cast (abstain is a sealed choice), so deposits and participation say nothing about turnout. Publish the winner only, not the tally. *(my reasoning)* | Small. Unanimity in a tiny group remains partly visible if the outcome itself is the same either way. |

## The tension to state plainly *(my reasoning)*

Shortfall 3 (let anyone verify the count) pulls against shortfall 5 (hide the tally). Publishing only the winner hides unanimity but makes the count unverifiable except by trusting attestation. Publishing the tally lets anyone check it but reveals unanimity. The workable middle for a committee: the winner goes on chain, the tally is revealed only to the roll members after the close, and two independent rooms must agree on it.

## Build order and the experiments each needs

1. **Signed ballots plus re-voting** (shortfalls 1, 4). Tests: an unsigned, wrongly signed, wrong-account, replayed or wrong-round ballot is refused; the latest ballot counts; mutation-tested.
2. **Ballot room program** (shortfall 5): roll, mandatory participation with abstain, quorum and majority, winner-only publication, the winner decides a payout between fixed options.
3. **Testnet comparison** against a public on-chain vote and an organizer-run poll, with predictions written first: votes an observer can infer, how far a last-mover can swing the result, double and outsider votes refused, exit if the room dies.
4. **Two-room agreement** (shortfall 3): contract change, then a run in which one room lies and the contract refuses.

## Not claimed

Coercion resistance beyond re-voting; one *person* one vote; a ZK-proved tally; any independent voters. All remain true after these fixes and will be written next to the results.
